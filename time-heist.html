Overview

Time Heist is a first-person stealth level set in a flooded cave ruin. On your first solo mission, an AI companion sends you to steal a relic before rival time bandits get to it.

You teleport onto pillars and rock ledges above the guards, freeze anyone who spots you with a time pistol, and set a time bomb that drains the water to reveal the ruins below, where the relic waits.

Level Design Class · 5 Check-ins

From Map to Finished Level

I built Time Heist for a level design class, presenting a new build at each check-in.

01

Plan it in 2D

Level design doc

I mapped the level on two floors: sea level, where the player starts, and below sea level, which opens once the water drains. Height is color-coded from -900 to 900 units, and each map shows the critical path, enemy patrols, and interactables.

02

Whitebox the route

Check-ins 2-3

I blocked the level out with gray shapes and placeholder guards. Between check-ins, I made it more vertical, adding ledges to teleport onto so players could feel danger below them without being punished for experimenting.

03

Art pass

Check-in 4

I replaced the whitebox with assets from one Fab marketplace pack, turning the gray blocks into a sunken ruin of arches and broken pillars.

04

Light and finish

Check-in 5 & final

I added lighting, torches, and the HUD. My plan called for a warm-to-cool day-to-night shift, but I cut it to keep the level readable, since my lighting skills were still early.

Gameplay

Mechanics & Decisions

The tools I gave the player and why.

Movement · Verticality

The safest route is always up.

The mechanic

Aim at a nearby ledge or pillar and teleport to it. It's on a cooldown, so each jump is a choice.

Why

The critical path runs over the guards' heads. Players can see the danger below them, but a missed jump isn't fatal, so they're free to experiment with routes.

Stealth tool

Freeze them before they find you.

The mechanic

The time pistol fires a laser that freezes a guard in place until they burn out of existence. An overheat meter limits how often you can use it.

Why

It's a stealth game, so the pistol is a way out of a mistake, not a weapon to clear the level. The cooldown keeps sneaking as the main plan.

Level structure

One level, two halves.

The mechanic

The first half ends at the time bomb. Setting it drains the water and reveals the ruins below sea level, where the relic is hidden.

Why

The water works as a barrier that stops the player from falling and guides them forward. Draining it reuses the same space as a whole new level beneath the first.

Scope · Time constraint

Ship what works, cut what doesn't.

The problem

The planned day-to-night lighting shift made the level too dark to read, and I didn't yet understand lighting well enough to fix it in time.

What I did

I cut it and kept one readable lighting setup, so I could focus my remaining time on the route and mechanics. The project earned a 100.

Reflection

This was one of my first levels. If I built it today, I'd tighten the pacing, make the AI smarter than spot-and-charge, and make the level bigger with more variety. Working against a deadline taught me to make the calls that get a level finished.
